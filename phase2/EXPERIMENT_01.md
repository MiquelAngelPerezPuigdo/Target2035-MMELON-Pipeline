# First Active Learning model comparison

This experiment checks transfer of the recovered DEL models to the organizer-released assay labels, compares release-label adaptation under chemistry-grouped holdouts, and uses the resulting rankings to construct validation panels. The holdouts are an internal diagnostic on a participant-selected set, not an estimate of performance in the remaining test library.

## Evidence and grouping

The release has 6,580 participant-selected compounds: 39 binders and 6,541 non-binders (0.593% within this selected release). All 100 prior selected test negatives are among those official labels. This fraction does not estimate hit prevalence among the 178,052 remaining test candidates.

ECFP4 Butina grouping at Tanimoto ≥0.65 produced 5,466 groups. Five stratified group folds held out 6–10 binders each. Related structures assigned to one Butina cluster stayed together. ECFP groups guard against close-analogue leakage; they are not the organizers' chemical-series labels.

## Held-out model comparison

Each value averages five held-out folds. The final column totals binders in each fold’s top 50 ranking, for 250 selections across five folds. It is a comparison within this release, not a forecast for a single 50-molecule test submission.

| Method | ROC AUC | Average precision | Trapezoidal PR AUC | Hits in fold top 50 (of 250) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Frozen DEL ECFP | 0.715 | 0.0198 | 0.0149 | 4/250 |
| Frozen DEL FCFP + descriptors | 0.658 | 0.0204 | 0.0141 | 1/250 |
| Historical G: ligand prior + DEL | 0.534 | 0.0098 | 0.0077 | 2/250 |
| Released-label neighborhood | 0.918 | 0.3579 | 0.3345 | 27/250 |
| DEL + released-label adaptation | 0.923 | 0.3439 | 0.3205 | 27/250 |

The experimental-neighborhood comparator ranked 27 binders in those 250 held-out picks. Adding frozen DEL logits and the ligand prior also ranked 27; average precision and trapezoidal PR AUC were lower, while ROC AUC was slightly higher. DEL adaptation therefore did not improve the key top-50 recovery measure in this diagnostic. It remains important to compare explicit DEL-led methods in validation, and any final workflow still needs significant DEL use.

For context, applying fixed phase-one rankings to the full labeled release recovered 1 of 39 binders in the top 50 with frozen ECFP DEL, 1 with frozen FCFP DEL, and 0 with historical ligand-led G. These are retrospective ranks on the selected feedback set; they do not measure expected hits in the remaining test pool.

## Validation panels prepared

Three local lists contain 50 unique validation CatalogIDs each, with no within-list ECFP4 pair above 0.65 Tanimoto and at most two molecules per exact Murcko scaffold. The panels overlap by 0 (DEL vs adaptation), 0 (DEL vs disagreement), and 2 (adaptation vs disagreement). They separate DEL support, label adaptation, and high adaptation score with weak DEL rank. These are for validation feedback and review, not blind-test files.

The combined local archive is [active_learning_validation_panels.zip](submissions/panels/active_learning_validation_panels.zip); the current bundle and individual submitted-list hashes are recorded in `reports/validation_panels.json`. The three 50-ID files are in `phase2/submissions/panels/`; detail CSVs provide their model ranks and structures. They were uploaded to the challenge project and submitted through the **Blind validation** queue: DEL support (`syn77821634`, submission `9783554`), Experimental adaptation (`syn77821633`, submission `9783555`), and Model disagreement (`syn77821632`, submission `9783556`). All three scored **0 hits**. Two panels overlap on two IDs, so this is 148 distinct candidates with zero recovered hits. The scores and email evidence are tracked in `status.json`.

A fourth panel uses 60% of the historical G ligand rank and 40% of mean ECFP/FCFP DEL rank, with all 1,363 previously selected IDs excluded. It satisfies the same 50-ID format and diversity checks, was uploaded as `syn77821898`, and scored zero as Blind validation submission `9783557`. This fresh-list result rules out duplicate candidates as the sole explanation. Its builder, list, details, and receipt are in [DEL_ligand_consensus](submissions/panels/DEL_ligand_consensus/manifest.json).

The Results page provides the complete earlier validation record and maps submission IDs to uploaded panel names. Across 51 phase-one evaluations, 19 scored zero and 32 found hits, totaling 163 hits. The most productive panels were ligand-informed: G (known-ligand prior plus DEL) found 25 hits in 3 series, K (new cores) 21 in 2 series, and M (K-bag partition) 21 in 2 series. This changes the diagnosis: validation history was mixed and included strong results, while the three current, more broadly model-driven panels all failed. The current rankings are not supported for another submission. Audit historical hitlist evidence, split and identifier handling, score calibration, and candidate coverage before selecting again. The mapped record is [validation_history.json](reports/validation_history.json); the phase-one ledger now also includes Synapse submission IDs.

Comparing current files against the 48 archived round panels finds that 32 of 50 DEL-support IDs had already been tested. Twenty-nine are logically forced negatives because every prior panel containing them scored zero; the remaining three have unresolved aggregate labels. The other two current panels contain no IDs from those archives. None of the 150 current rows selected any of the eight inferable positive validation labels. This pinpoints a feedback-filtering omission in DEL-support and poor recovery of historical positives across the current methods. It does not show that all 29 repeated negatives caused the zero panel score, nor does the eight-positive sample support an independent generalization claim. See [validation_panel_history_overlap.json](reports/validation_panel_history_overlap.json).

An exact binary-integer feasibility check used all 48 archived panel hit counts together, testing both possible labels for each of the 611 unresolved IDs. It proved two more negatives, Z31233864 and Z31246525, leaving 8 forced positives, 630 forced negatives, and 609 unresolved IDs. No additional positives were identified, so this does not change the model-transfer diagnosis. The derivation is reproducible with `python phase2/infer_validation_labels_milp.py`; see [validation_label_milp.json](reports/validation_label_milp.json). These remain adaptive feedback labels, not an independent benchmark.

A second audit tested whether a DEL fingerprint model trained on exact historic panel counts could recover high-performing validation chemistry. Each held-out panel and every other panel sharing any of its IDs were removed before recomputing training-only forced labels. The model predicted only 4.7–6.0 hits for G (observed 25) and 10.7–12.1 for K (observed 21), across ECFP and FCFP. It matched the smaller O and AI panels more closely, but overpredicted two zero-hit panels. The earlier exploratory audit did not fully recompute propagated labels inside each fold; this corrected audit supersedes it. These six adaptively chosen panels are a diagnostic, not independent validation. See [historical_bag_transfer_leakage_audit.json](reports/historical_bag_transfer_leakage_audit.json) and reproduce it with `python phase2/audit_historical_bags_leakage.py`.

A fixed-weight sweep on the 39 released binders' chemistry-grouped OOF scores compared their experimental-neighborhood ranks with frozen DEL, the historical G score, and the DEL-plus-release adapter. No blend improved on the neighborhood-only result of 27 hits in 250 held-out top-50 selections. Small DEL weights sometimes slightly improved full-fold AP/AUC while reducing or leaving top-50 recovery unchanged; stronger DEL weights reduced recovery. Thus the current release supports DEL only as a secondary ranking signal, not as demonstrated top-50 uplift. Do not infer a strong DEL-led test ranking from these selected-sample results. See [release_del_blend_audit.json](reports/release_del_blend_audit.json); reproduce it with `python phase2/audit_release_del_blend.py`.

An exact provenance audit resolved the possible row-mapping concern: the validation CSV and feature metadata match row for row; all 148 selected structures reproduce their cached fingerprints and feature-matrix rows; and recomputed frozen-model predictions for those rows match the stored per-seed and mean scores exactly. A score-to-CatalogID indexing error is therefore unsupported as an explanation. See [validation_row_provenance_audit.json](reports/validation_row_provenance_audit.json).

Scoring the 48 archived phase-one lists with current full-library rankings reveals a mismatch with the most successful panels. G, K, and M returned 25, 21, and 21 hits, yet their median released-label-adaptation rank fractions are 0.09, 0.30, and 0.39 (higher is better). Their median frozen-DEL rank fractions are 0.87, 0.80, and 0.85. This suggests the adaptation learned from the 39 selected test binders shifts rankings away from earlier ligand-informed validation chemistry, while frozen DEL preserves more of that signal. This comparison is exploratory: successful panels have aggregate hit counts, not molecule-level labels, and their candidates were adaptively selected. Per-panel rank metrics are in [validation_panels.json](reports/validation_panels.json).

The released test binders also transfer poorly to the inferable historical validation binders by direct ECFP4 similarity. Maximum similarity to any of the 39 released binders had ROC AUC 0.244 for the eight forced validation positives versus 628 forced negatives; median similarity was 0.182 for positives and 0.213 for negatives. The phase-two adapter's standardized coefficients are negative for ECFP-DEL (-0.136) and public-ligand similarity (-0.134), while released-label neighborhood terms dominate. Public-ligand similarity alone puts one of the eight inferable validation positives in its top 50; the historical G formula (95% ligand prior plus 5% ECFP-DEL) puts one in its top 500 but none in its top 50; the adapter puts none in its top 500. This small adaptive sample is not an independent benchmark, but it points to the next offline comparison: preserve the existing ligand/DEL signal and test released-label neighborhoods as secondary evidence. Treat the 39 released binders as a selected, chemistry-biased seed set, not as transferable nearest-neighbor exemplars for validation. Details and source hashes are in [validation_release_transfer.json](reports/validation_release_transfer.json) and [validation_panels.json](reports/validation_panels.json).

## Decision and limits

All four current ranking panels failed their external check, but the complete 51-submission phase-one record is not a run of zero-hit results. Reuse its per-panel hit counts and archived candidate lists as adaptive evidence, keeping aggregate bag constraints distinct from molecule-level labels. The eight forced positive validation labels are too sparse to stand in for all prior feedback. The fresh ligand/DEL panel also failed after excluding all earlier candidates, so stop spending validation credits until a stronger transfer hypothesis is developed. Any eventual final ranking must remain meaningfully informed by DEL models or DEL-derived evidence and show that contribution through a removal ablation.

The official series assignments were not released, so local ECFP/Murcko diversity is only a selection heuristic. The 39 positives were selected by participants, the 100 historical negatives overlap this release, and the label neighborhoods may identify chemistry favored by prior participant rankings. The validation list feedback is adaptive and should be declared in the writeup. The account's total submission history and remaining challenge allowance are not yet reconciled.
